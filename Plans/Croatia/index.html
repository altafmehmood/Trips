✈️ Your Aircraft: Aviation Geek Edition

🛫 Boeing 777-200 (UA 46 & UA 47)

The 777-200 is Boeing's twin-engine widebody workhorse — the aircraft that proved twin-engine jets could rule long-haul routes that once required three or four engines. Powered by the GE90-94B (the world's most powerful commercial engine at entry into service), each engine produces 94,000 lbf of thrust.

Tech Highlights: ETOPS-180 certified, allowing it to fly routes 180 minutes from the nearest diversion airport. The distinctive large oval fuselage cross-section (239 inches wide) enables nine-abreast seating. The 777's fly-by-wire system with a conventional control column (not sidestick) was Boeing's first full FBW design.

Stats: Range: 5,235 nm (9,695 km) | Cruise Speed: Mach 0.84 | Length: 209 ft | Wingspan: 199 ft 11 in | First Flight: June 1994

Fun Fact: The GE90 fan blades are the largest composite fan blades on any commercial engine — each one larger than a typical carry-on bag at 128 inches in diameter.

✈️ Airbus A321neo (LH 1710) & A220-300 (OU 416)

The A321neo's CFM LEAP-1A delivers a 20% fuel burn improvement over previous-generation narrowbodies. The approach into Dubrovnik is one of Europe's most dramatic — a steep descent over the Dinaric Alps with the Adriatic suddenly appearing below the wingtip. The A220-300 (originally Bombardier C Series) is the smallest aircraft on this trip: a 2-3 cabin design with Pratt & Whitney PW1500G geared turbofans.

A321neo Stats: Range: 4,000 nm (7,400 km) | Cruise: Mach 0.78 | Length: 146 ft | First Flight: Feb 2016

A220-300 Stats: Range: 3,400 nm (6,300 km) | Cruise: Mach 0.82 | Length: 127 ft | Seats: up to 160 (2-3 config)

Fun Fact: The A220's PW1500G gearbox spins the fan 3× slower than the core — dramatically reducing noise and improving efficiency. The A220 program was acquired by Airbus in 2018 after Bombardier sold a majority stake.
