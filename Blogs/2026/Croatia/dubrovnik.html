🏰 Dubrovnik

Days 1–3 • April 25–27, 2026 • Pearl of the Adriatic

Day 1: Arrival in Dubrovnik
Saturday, April 25 • First glimpse of the walled city

After an overnight flight from Houston via London, we landed at Dubrovnik Airport on a warm, sun-drenched Saturday afternoon. The limestone hills rolled down to the sea on both sides of the runway and the Adriatic glittered somewhere below the clouds — a first hint of what the next few days held.

We checked into Sun Gardens Dubrovnik in the village of Orašac, a five-star resort perched on the coastal slopes fifteen minutes north of the Old Town. The drive in from the airport along the Jadranska magistrala coastal road gave us a first glimpse of the fortified city rising from the sea — all red rooftops, pale stone towers, and crystalline water — and the jet lag evaporated on the spot.

That first evening we took the hotel shuttle into the Old Town for dinner. Walking through the Pile Gate for the first time as dusk settled over the Stradun was quietly overwhelming. The street glows amber at golden hour; the smooth limestone flags underfoot are polished by eight centuries of footfall; the Adriatic winks at the end of every side alley. We ate well, walked slowly, and went to bed early.

Sun Gardens Dubrovnik: A Leading Hotels of the World property with a private pebble beach, four pools (one indoor), and a complimentary shuttle to the Old Town. The position above the coast, set into natural Mediterranean woodland, makes for one of the most beautiful hotel arrivals in the region.
Day 2: Mount Srđ & The City Walls
Sunday, April 26 • Sunset from the summit

Mount Srđ dominates the skyline above Dubrovnik from every angle — the bare limestone ridge that sheltered the city during the 1991–92 siege, and now home to a cable car that whisks visitors 400 metres straight up to the summit. We took the hike instead, working our way up the steep switchbacks in the morning cool to earn views that opened progressively with every bend in the path.

Nothing prepares you for the view from the top. The walled city spreads out below in perfect geometry — a terracotta-roofed grid locked inside perfectly intact medieval battlements, surrounded entirely by the deepest blue we'd seen outside a paint tin. The Elafiti island archipelago stretches away to the northwest in a dissolving chain. The open Adriatic glitters to the south. Somewhere far out, Italy.

We spent the afternoon walking the famous city walls themselves — a 2km circuit around the entire perimeter of the Old Town that took about ninety minutes at an easy pace and offered a completely different perspective from above the rooftops. The afternoon light turned the stone honey-warm and the sea a deepening shade of sapphire.

The real revelation came in the evening. We waited on Srđ for sunset — and understood immediately why people describe it as one of the great sunsets in the world. The Elafiti islands dissolved into crimson and amber. The Old Town below turned from pale gold to burnished copper. The cable car ran its last gondola down as the last light faded over the sea and the stars came out over the Adriatic. One of those views you carry home inside you.

The City Walls

The 2km walk around Dubrovnik's city walls is rightly one of the most celebrated short walks in Europe. Built between the 13th and 16th centuries, the walls stand up to 6 metres thick in places and rise to 25 metres on the landward side. The circuit offers a constantly shifting perspective — one moment looking inward over the red-tiled rooftops to the Stradun, the next peering outward over the sea cliffs to the Adriatic below.

St John's Fort anchors the southern end of the harbour with particular drama, and the stretch around the eastern seaward wall — where the battlements run almost directly above the waterline — has a vertiginous quality that keeps you close to the outer railing. On a warm spring afternoon with the sea a luminous blue-green, it's close to perfection.

Day 3: Old Town & Departure
Monday, April 27 • Lovrijenac Fort and the Old Harbour

Our last morning in Dubrovnik was spent on the western approaches to the Old Town — the side that most visitors arrive at through the Pile Gate but fewer linger in. Lovrijenac Fort, the so-called Gibraltar of Dubrovnik, perches on a sheer 37-metre cliff immediately outside the city walls and was built in the 11th century specifically to deny control of the harbour to any potential attacker. Its Latin motto above the main gate — "Non bene pro toto libertas venditur auro" (Freedom is not sold for all the gold in the world) — tells you everything about the Ragusan Republic's priorities.

The Old Harbour, tucked inside the eastern walls, was our final stop before picking up the hire car and heading north. Small fishing boats bobbed alongside tour vessels in the morning light; the walls of the Fort of St John cast dramatic shadows across the water. After two days in this extraordinary city, leaving felt like a minor injustice.
