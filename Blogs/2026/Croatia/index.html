🗺️ The Route: Dubrovnik to Zagreb

A reverse south-to-north road trip along the Croatian coast and into the heart of Slovenia

The Flights: Houston → Frankfurt → Dubrovnik
United & Lufthansa • 5 flights • 4 aircraft types • 3 countries

This trip had an unusual shape: it didn't begin in Croatia. A work conference in Frankfurt meant crossing the Atlantic six days before setting foot on the Dalmatian Coast — which turned out to be a rather elegant way to start a European adventure. The outbound leg on Sunday 19 April was United 46, Houston IAH to Frankfurt FRA, aboard a Boeing 777-200. United operates this route with its "Overseas" B772 configuration — a 3-3-3 economy layout that still gives the cabin a proper wide-body feel. The 777-200's GE90-94B engines are among the most powerful ever certified on a commercial aircraft, each producing up to 94,000 lbf of thrust; the pair of them carry a fully-loaded 777 across the Atlantic with ETOPS authority that makes three- and four-engine jets look redundant. Departed Gate E4 at 06:02PM CDT, touched down Frankfurt at 10:43AM CEST the following morning — 32 minutes early after a great-circle arc that grazes the Labrador coast and tracks northeast across Ireland.

Six days of conference later, it was time to pivot from work to holiday. On Saturday 25 April, Lufthansa 106 operated the Frankfurt–Munich segment aboard a Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner — one of aviation's great mismatches of aircraft to route. A 787-9 on a 45-minute domestic hop is the aeronautical equivalent of using a sledgehammer to crack a nut: the aircraft is designed for ultra-long-haul routes up to 7,530 nautical miles, uses carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer for over 50% of its structure, and can pressurise its cabin to a 6,000ft equivalent (versus the industry-standard 8,000ft) to reduce passenger fatigue. All of that engineering for a 116-mile hop over Bavaria. Lufthansa likely rostered it as a positioning or through-service leg. Departed FRA 12:33PM, gate arrival Munich 01:22PM — and then straight onto Lufthansa 1710.

LH1710 was the flight that actually started the holiday: Munich to Dubrovnik aboard an Airbus A321neo. The "neo" (New Engine Option) designation means CFM LEAP-1A engines delivering 15–20% better fuel efficiency than the previous-generation ceo. Airbus launched the A321neo family in 2017 and it has since become the workhorse of European mid-range routes. The 1h 26m flight crosses Austria, skirts the northern Adriatic, and drops down through the Dinaric Alps — a genuinely scenic approach. Gate departure Munich 02:42PM CEST, gate arrival Dubrovnik 04:08PM CEST, 1 minute early.

The return on Sunday 3 May was a study in aircraft diversity. Lufthansa 6001, operated by Croatia Airlines as flight OU416, flew Zagreb to Frankfurt aboard an Airbus A220-300 — arguably the most interesting aircraft of the whole trip. The A220 started life as the Bombardier CSeries CS300, designed in Montréal and powered by Pratt & Whitney PW1500G geared turbofan engines. Airbus acquired a majority stake in 2018 and rebranded it; Croatia Airlines operates a small fleet of them on regional European routes. The A220-300's 2-3 cabin layout means every passenger gets either a window or an aisle — no middle seats — which makes it unusually pleasant for a narrowbody. Departed Zagreb 08:50AM, arrived Frankfurt 10:09AM, on time. Then after a few hours at FRA, United 47 — the mirror image of the outbound — brought the 777-200 back into play for the 10h 47m westbound Atlantic crossing, fighting headwinds all the way home. Departed Frankfurt 01:44PM CEST, touched down Houston 05:31PM CDT, a very satisfying 29 minutes early.

Final Reflections

Croatia has a way of exceeding even generous expectations. We arrived primed for the coastline and the history — we didn't anticipate quite how completely the national parks would steal the show. Plitvice Lakes and Krka NP, both visited at peak spring flow with melting snowpack feeding every waterfall, were among the most visually astonishing places either of us has ever walked through. The turquoise water is genuinely that colour; no filter required, no hyperbole needed.

Dubrovnik, for all the tourist noise surrounding it, still managed to deliver moments of pure magic. Watching the sun slide into the Adriatic from the summit of Mount Srđ — the walled city glowing amber below, the Elafiti islands dissolving into crimson haze — is the kind of view that makes you understand why people call this place extraordinary. Walking the city walls at golden hour, with the limestone towers and terracotta rooftops on one side and open sea on the other, remains a highlight of the entire trip.

Slovenia was the unexpected bonus. Lake Bled is every bit the fairy-tale it promises: the island church, the clifftop castle, the Pletna rowing boats, the Julian Alps reflected in emerald water. We squeezed in a detour to Lake Bohinj and found it even more quietly beautiful — bigger, wilder, and almost entirely free of the crowds. The drive through the Alps from Plitvice to Bled through spring-green mountain passes was itself one of the day's highlights.

Ten days was enough to feel the shape of these two countries without rushing. We'd go back tomorrow.
